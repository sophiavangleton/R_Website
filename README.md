# Cognitive Dynamics Lab R Lessons Series

This github repo houses all materials for the website used in the 7 week series of r lessons for the research assistants in the [Cognitive Dynamics Laboratory](https://blogs.uoregon.edu/cognitivedynamics/home/) at the University of Oregon. This website is a compilation of all the scripts revised over the years created and taught by the various graduate students in the Cognitive Dynamics Lab. I made this website as a place for all these files to live in a more straightforward way, and added some clarifying elements + new lessons to each weeks content. This site is made to be updated as the way we use R inevitably changes, so please copy this repo and update the site when needed!

Within this repository you can find all the files we will use for this course, and all relevent links to each weeks content are also downloadable on the website. In addition to the weekly lessons that are made to be taught in-person, there are a number of resources for independent work. Output of the site (all renderings and files for download) are found in the 'Docs' folder. 

## This Websites Features:

**CAT Quiz**
- This houses a large quiz that is geared for personalized individual learning of the basic in R. This quiz uses CAT or computerized adaptive testing so that the series of questions are guided by your skill level. Students should take this quiz throughout the course to gauge where they are in their experience level.

**Weekly Lessons Tabs**
- Contains the meat and potatoes of this website, it is all the lessons for this course. On the top of each weekly lesson, there is a downloadable .qmd for students to download and follow along in-person and any additional files they may need for the week. All downloadable student ready .qmds can be found in the studentversion folder on the repo. 

**The Big Wide World of R Page:**
  - This page on the website houses external resources for building R knowlege both on and off campus. Here I will also discuss practical use of GenAI when you are learning (which is to say please let yourself learn something before you offload it to something else). 

## Notes For Teachers: 
From previous experience teaching this course, its recommended to budget out 7 weeks to get through all content, with **1 weekly in-person lesson with 1.5-2 hours** budgeted to get through content and have students complete meaningful examples. Each lesson should take ~1 hour to walk through, and budget ~30-45 minutes at the end of each session for students to complete the examples provided at the end of each week on their own but still in a group enviroment. 

**Things to emphasize at the START!**
This is layed out in the 'start here' tab and is the first page when you're on the website. From my experience, reiterate to students for the first few weeks 1. how to open their project, 2. how to check if their weekly scripts they downloaded and any datafiles they may need are housed in said project. The other main thing is make sure they understand how to name files correctly (no spaces) and emphasize that at the start to reduce further revisions.
